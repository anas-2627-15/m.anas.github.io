##  Understanding Digital Logic Design and Circuit Implementation

During my early semesters in Computer Engineering, I was introduced to Digital Logic Design (DLD), which focuses on how digital systems are built using basic electronic components. This subject helped me understand how hardware works behind software.

At first, logic gates and circuit design felt complex, but with continuous practice, I gradually developed a clear understanding.

---

##  Learning Through Practice
In DLD, I learned about fundamental components such as:

- AND Gate  
- OR Gate  
- NOT Gate  
- NAND Gate  
- NOR Gate  

These gates are the building blocks of digital systems.

I also used **Microwind software** to design and simulate circuits. This helped me visualize how circuits behave with different inputs.

---

##  Logic Design Example
```
Output = (A AND B) OR (NOT C)
```
## Explanation

This expression shows how multiple logic gates can be combined to perform decision-making operations. Using Microwind, I tested different inputs and observed how the output changes.

By implementing such logic in simulation, I understood how digital circuits process signals step by step. This also helped me see how simple logical expressions can be used to build more advanced systems like processors and memory units.

## Reflection

Learning DLD gave me a strong foundation in hardware concepts. It helped me understand how software interacts with hardware and improved my logical thinking.

This experience also improved my problem-solving approach, as I learned how to break complex circuits into smaller parts. It gave me confidence to work on more advanced topics related to computer architecture and embedded systems in the future.
